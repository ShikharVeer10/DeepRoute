Department of Computer Science and Engineering (Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning)
[3] Abstract
[4] The exponential growth of urban populations, increasing vehicle density, and rapid expansion of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) have significantly increased the complexity of modern route planning. Conventional navigation systems predominantly rely on deterministic shortest-path algorithms such as Dijkstra's and A* search, which optimize routes using static edge weights based on travel distance or estimated travel time. While these algorithms provide computationally efficient solutions, they are unable to effectively model the dynamic and uncertain characteristics of real-world transportation environments, including fluctuating traffic congestion, adverse weather conditions, road incidents, construction activities, environmental risks, and temporal travel variations. Consequently, traditional routing techniques often generate suboptimal navigation decisions that increase travel time, reduce route reliability, and limit transportation efficiency.
[5] To address these challenges, this paper presents DeepRoute, an intelligent machine learning-based route planning framework that integrates predictive analytics, graph-based optimization, and context-aware decision-making into a unified and scalable architecture. The proposed framework models transportation networks as directed weighted graphs constructed from OpenStreetMap (OSM) road data while incorporating heterogeneous information sources including traffic congestion, weather conditions, road geometry, historical travel behaviour, and contextual risk indicators. A comprehensive feature engineering pipeline transforms these heterogeneous inputs into structured temporal, spatial, and contextual feature representations suitable for predictive learning. Temporal information such as hour of day, weekday, and peak-hour patterns are encoded alongside spatial attributes including road length, lane count, speed limits, and road hierarchy, while contextual features capture traffic density, precipitation, road closures, accident reports, and environmental risks.
[6] The predictive intelligence of DeepRoute is driven by Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) to estimate dynamic travel-time factors, enabling the framework to replace conventional static edge weights with adaptive, data-driven routing costs. These predicted travel costs are integrated into a multi-objective routing engine capable of simultaneously optimizing travel time, congestion, route safety, environmental impact, route reliability, and driving comfort. The modular architecture further supports uncertainty-aware route planning through stochastic evaluation strategies and is deployed using a FastAPI-based REST architecture that enables real-time route planning, ETA prediction, route recommendation, and intelligent transportation services.
[7] Compared with conventional shortest-path routing algorithms, DeepRoute extends navigation beyond deterministic distance optimization by integrating predictive machine learning, contextual feature engineering, adaptive edge weighting, and multi-objective graph optimization within a unified framework. The proposed architecture provides a scalable foundation for next-generation Intelligent Transportation Systems, smart-city infrastructure, logistics optimization, autonomous mobility, and data-driven navigation services while demonstrating the practical integration of artificial intelligence and graph analytics for intelligent route planning.

[12] I. Introduction
[13] A. Background
[14] Transportation networks have evolved into highly complex cyber-physical systems where mobility decisions must continuously adapt to dynamic environmental conditions. Rapid urbanization, increasing vehicle ownership, population growth, and expanding logistics operations have substantially increased traffic congestion and travel uncertainty across metropolitan regions. These challenges have accelerated the development of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), which combine sensing technologies, communication infrastructures, geographic information systems, optimization algorithms, and artificial intelligence to improve transportation efficiency and commuter safety.
[15] Route planning represents one of the fundamental services within modern ITS. Navigation systems attempt to determine an optimal path between a source and destination while minimizing one or more objectives such as travel time, travel distance, operational cost, fuel consumption, or environmental impact. Classical graph-search algorithms, including Dijkstra's Algorithm, Bellman-Ford Algorithm, Floyd-Warshall Algorithm, and A* Search, have served as the foundation of navigation systems for several decades due to their mathematical guarantees and computational efficiency.
[16] Despite their widespread adoption, these algorithms assume that edge weights remain static throughout route computation. In practical transportation environments, however, edge weights continuously change as traffic congestion evolves, weather conditions deteriorate, accidents occur, construction zones emerge, or road capacities fluctuate. Consequently, deterministic shortest-path algorithms frequently fail to recommend routes that accurately reflect current transportation conditions.
[18] B. Motivation
[19] Recent developments in Machine Learning and Artificial Intelligence have introduced predictive capabilities into transportation research, enabling navigation systems to estimate future traffic behaviour instead of relying exclusively on current observations. Travel-time prediction models can exploit historical traffic patterns, temporal trends, spatial road characteristics, and contextual environmental information to generate significantly more accurate travel-time estimates.
[20] However, many existing approaches address only isolated aspects of intelligent routing. Some studies focus exclusively on travel-time prediction without integrating predictive outputs into graph optimization, while others improve graph search algorithms but ignore predictive analytics and uncertainty modelling. Furthermore, relatively few intelligent routing frameworks provide scalable deployment architectures capable of supporting real-time navigation services.
[21] DeepRoute has been developed to bridge this gap by integrating predictive machine learning, feature engineering, graph analytics, and multi-objective optimization into a single intelligent routing framework capable of supporting real-time route planning within Intelligent Transportation Systems.
[23] C. Research Problem
[24] Current navigation systems compute optimal routes primarily using deterministic graph-search techniques that assign fixed weights to road segments. Such approaches fail to account for continuously changing transportation conditions including traffic density, weather disruptions, road incidents, construction activities, and temporal travel behaviour.
[25] The central research problem addressed in this work is therefore:
[26] How can machine learning, graph analytics, and contextual feature engineering be integrated to develop an intelligent routing framework capable of generating adaptive, reliable, and context-aware route recommendations under dynamic transportation conditions?
[27] To solve this problem, DeepRoute replaces static edge weighting with predictive travel-time estimation derived from engineered temporal, spatial, and contextual features. These adaptive edge costs are subsequently optimized using graph-search techniques capable of balancing multiple routing objectives.
[29] D. Objectives
[30] The primary objectives of this research are:
[31] To develop a scalable machine learning-based route planning framework capable of supporting Intelligent Transportation Systems. 
[32] To construct dynamic road-network representations enriched with traffic, weather, temporal, spatial, and contextual information. 
[33] To design an effective feature engineering pipeline that captures transportation dynamics influencing travel behaviour. 
[34] To employ predictive machine learning models for accurate travel-time estimation across road segments. 
[35] To integrate predicted travel times into a graph-based routing engine capable of generating adaptive route recommendations. 
[36] To support multi-objective route optimization considering travel time, congestion, safety, route reliability, and contextual transportation conditions. 
[37] To provide a modular deployment architecture suitable for real-time intelligent transportation applications through scalable REST APIs. 
[39] E. Scope of the Proposed Work
[40] DeepRoute is designed as a modular intelligent routing platform that combines artificial intelligence, predictive analytics, graph optimization, and modern software engineering practices. The framework supports urban navigation, logistics optimization, emergency response planning, fleet management, and future smart-city transportation services. Its layered architecture enables future integration of additional predictive models, graph neural networks, reinforcement learning techniques, and real-time transportation APIs without major architectural modifications.
[41] Unlike conventional navigation systems that optimize only static shortest paths, DeepRoute establishes a flexible foundation for intelligent transportation by integrating adaptive travel-time prediction, contextual decision-making, and scalable deployment into a unified routing framework.

[60] III. Related Work
[61] Route planning has evolved significantly over the past two decades due to advances in graph theory, optimization algorithms, machine learning, and artificial intelligence. Traditional navigation systems primarily focused on computing shortest paths, whereas modern Intelligent Transportation Systems emphasize predictive analytics, adaptive routing, and multi-objective optimization.
[62] A. Classical Shortest Path Algorithms
[63] The earliest navigation systems were based on graph-search algorithms such as Dijkstra's Algorithm, Bellman-Ford, and Floyd-Warshall. These algorithms guarantee mathematically optimal shortest paths under deterministic edge weights. Although computationally efficient, they assume static transportation conditions and therefore cannot adapt to real-time traffic congestion or changing environmental factors.
[64] B. Heuristic Routing
[65] The A* algorithm introduced heuristic search to reduce computational complexity while maintaining near-optimal routing performance. A* significantly improves execution speed by guiding graph exploration toward the destination using heuristic functions. However, similar to Dijkstra's algorithm, it still depends on static edge weights and cannot predict future transportation conditions.
[66] C. Machine Learning-Based Travel Time Prediction
[67] Recent research has demonstrated the effectiveness of machine learning algorithms for estimating travel times using historical traffic observations, weather conditions, and contextual road information. Gradient Boosting techniques such as XGBoost have become particularly popular due to their ability to model nonlinear relationships while maintaining high prediction accuracy and computational efficiency.
[68] D. Graph Neural Networks
[69] Graph Neural Networks (GNNs), including Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) and Graph Attention Networks (GATs), have recently emerged as powerful approaches for modelling transportation networks because they preserve spatial relationships between neighbouring road segments. These methods enable richer spatial representations than conventional machine learning algorithms.
[70] E. Multi-Objective Route Planning
[71] Real-world transportation systems require simultaneous optimization of several conflicting objectives including travel time, congestion, emissions, safety, fuel consumption, and passenger comfort. Evolutionary optimization techniques such as NSGA-II, Weighted Sum Models (WSM), and Pareto Optimization have therefore become increasingly popular for intelligent routing applications.
[72] F. Risk-Aware Routing
[73] Traditional navigation systems optimize expected travel time but rarely account for uncertainty. Recent studies have introduced stochastic optimization methods using Monte Carlo simulation together with Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) to evaluate routing robustness under uncertain transportation conditions. These techniques improve route reliability by minimizing the probability of severe travel delays.
[75] Comparison of Existing Literature
[77] Research Gap
[78] Although considerable research has been conducted in intelligent transportation systems, several important challenges remain unresolved.
[79] Most shortest-path algorithms assume deterministic edge weights and therefore fail to adapt to changing traffic conditions.
[80] Machine learning models often focus only on travel-time prediction without integrating predictions into routing algorithms.
[81] Graph Neural Networks provide strong spatial learning capabilities but generally lack integrated deployment architectures suitable for real-time navigation.
[82] Multi-objective optimization techniques frequently ignore uncertainty associated with dynamic transportation environments.
[83] Finally, very few routing frameworks combine predictive machine learning, graph optimization, uncertainty modelling, and scalable REST-based deployment within a unified intelligent transportation platform.
[84] DeepRoute addresses these research gaps by integrating predictive travel-time estimation, contextual feature engineering, adaptive graph optimization, and modular software deployment into a single intelligent routing framework.

[280] •  Layer 1: Data Ingestion Layer: This layer is responsible for raw data collection and loading. It maps the local coordinate spaces onto a directed MultiDiGraph road network fetched from OpenStreetMap (OSM) via the OSMnx module. Concurrently, simulated live traffic conditions (congestion index, speeds, and incidents) and environmental variables (temperature, relative humidity, precipitation, wind speed) are ingested from weather and traffic loaders to represent current edge costs.
[281] •  Layer 2: Feature Engineering Layer: Ingested raw attributes are mapped into a structured schema containing temporal features (hour, weekday, weekend, and peak hour encodings utilizing cyclical sine/cosine representation), spatial features (road length, lane counts, speed limits, and elevation profiles), and context features (traffic density, precipitation level, active road closures, accidents, and construction). These features are aggregated into a single CombinedFeatureVector.
[282] •  Layer 3: Prediction Layer: The prediction engine utilizes either a high-capacity XGBoost regressor (700 estimators) or the hybrid Temporal-GAT model. The prediction target is a dynamic speed multiplier (travel time factor). Using the predicted factor, the engine estimates the calibrated travel duration across individual segments.
[283] •  Layer 4: Routing & Optimization Layer: Using the edge costs, multiple candidate paths are generated on the network graph using a Penalty-Based Rerouting algorithm, ensuring alternative path diversity. Candidates are scored using a pluggable Weighted Sum Model (WSM) multi-objective cost function balancing travel time, distance, comfort, emissions, EV energy, and risk. To quantify tail-end risk, a 1000-run Monte Carlo simulation calculates Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR).
[284] •  Layer 5: Application Layer: The application layer serves predictions via a high-performance, asynchronous FastAPI REST interface. The endpoints include GET /health, POST /route, POST /alternatives, POST /risk, and POST /recommend. Recommendations are rendered dynamically on an interactive Streamlit UI dashboard featuring a Leaflet map.
[288] VI. Proposed Work
[289] A. Overview
[290] DeepRoute proposes an intelligent, machine learning-driven route planning framework that combines predictive analytics, graph optimization, and modular software engineering to generate adaptive route recommendations under dynamic transportation conditions. Unlike conventional navigation systems that compute shortest paths using static edge weights, the proposed framework continuously estimates route costs based on predicted travel conditions, enabling more reliable navigation decisions.
[291] The proposed architecture consists of five interconnected layers that transform heterogeneous transportation data into intelligent route recommendations. The workflow begins with road network construction and contextual data collection, proceeds through feature engineering and machine learning-based travel-time prediction, and concludes with multi-objective route optimization and deployment through scalable REST APIs.
[293] B. Proposed Architecture
[294] The architecture consists of five sequential processing layers.
[295] The prediction layer (Layer 3) deploys a dual-pipeline inference engine. For high-speed tabular feature forecasting, it uses XGBoost (700 estimators). For relational, spatial-temporal graph representation, it executes the hybrid Temporal-GAT deep neural model. The routing layer (Layer 4) executes two key advancements: (1) a Penalty-Based Rerouting algorithm that dynamically inflates segment weights of computed routes, generating geometrically diverse alternative path candidates; and (2) a Monte Carlo simulation engine that propagates prediction variance to compute tail-end Risk and CVaR travel times.
